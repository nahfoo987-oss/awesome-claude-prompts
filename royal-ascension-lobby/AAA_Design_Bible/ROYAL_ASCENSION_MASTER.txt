Lobby Design & Build Master Document
Crown Chaos  ·  Production V3
Floating Corrupted Royal Fortress  ·  96×96 Studs  ·  28-Stud Ceiling
This document consolidates all design sessions: original spec, rebuild specification, material & texture system, Anime Awakening applied lessons, and lighting system. It is the single source of truth for the Royal Ascension lobby.
1. Project Overview
The Royal Ascension is the premium social lobby for Crown Chaos. It is a floating corrupted royal fortress suspended above an abyss, designed to make players feel like they are entering a serious competitive world built around status, spectacle, and the ambition to become king.
1.1 Emotional Goals
This game is serious.
This world is huge.
I want to become the king.
Everything here feels expensive.
1.2 Core Design Goals
Make the lobby feel like a royal fortress hub — not a messy sculpture pile.
One dominant center focal point, then 3–4 clearly separated wings.
Symmetrical or near-symmetrical layout so players instantly understand where to go.
Every key area visible from the spawn point — no navigation confusion.
Avoid overloading with too many tall objects, thin pillars, or random diagonal neon bars.
Every object must have a purpose: center, path, wing, landmark, portal, or decoration.
No simulator clutter. No random bright kiosks. Every feature looks like part of the royal fortress.

5.2 Built-In Materials — Skip SurfaceAppearance
Basalt: outer walls, tint #141414. Built-in pitted texture reads perfectly at fortress scale. SA wastes GPU here.
Fabric: banners, tint #4a0e0e. Built-in weave ideal for banners. Only add a crown decal on top.
Metal: railings and chains, tint #0e0e0e or #1e1410. Future lighting makes built-in Metal already look premium.
Neon: NEVER add SurfaceAppearance to Neon parts. Emissive material ignores all texture maps.

EnvironmentSpecularScale 0.08 is the single most critical number in the lighting setup. This is what causes blown-out white patches on gold and steel when set too high. Lock it at 0.08 before touching any texture.
6.3 The Proximity Rule
For every Part within 15 studs of a PointLight, subtract 0.06 from its Reflectance value. A gold trim at 0.18 in open air becomes 0.12 if it is near the crown beam PointLight.
Anti-reflective test method:
Place a temporary PointLight Brightness 4, Range 20 at the crown beam top.
Walk around the lobby. Any surface showing a pure white patch needs its Reflectance dropped.
Remove the test light after. This test must be done before final texture pass.
Two-material trick for arch frames:
Front face of arch frame (lit by SpotLight): #a88940 aged brass at Reflectance 0.10.
Sides and back face: #C7A65A at Reflectance 0.18.
Creates natural lit/shadow side without any extra lighting work.

8.3 What Replaces the Diagonal Neon Bars
Gold trim strips guide players: 1×0.5×16 gold SmoothPlastic strips along path edges. Slight reflectance catches ambient light. No neon needed.
Height changes replace neon markers: 2-stud raised hub, 1-stud raised pads, spawn dais already communicate 'important area.' Neon on top of raised geometry is redundant.
SpotLights replace ambient neon glow: SpotLights above each wing entrance, pointing down, color #C7A65A. A real directed light creates the 'special area' feeling better than scattered neon bars.

9.2 Decal Rules
Texture object for repeating patterns (floor marble). Decal for single centered images (spawn sigil, portal glyph). Wrong choice causes bad scaling.
Stone floor decals at transparency 0.1–0.2 so they blend in rather than float on top.
512×512 for close-range items (throne sigil, portal glyph). 256×256 fine for banners at medium distance.
Use SurfaceGui for interactive elements (leaderboard, shop signs) — lets scripts update content dynamically.
No floating text Parts anywhere in the 3D world. All text lives in SurfaceGui or BillboardGui.
Building labels go on SurfaceGui panels — never extruded letter Parts.
Use crown symbols, runes, and geometric crests instead of readable words on structure walls.

12. Build & Revamp Order
Lesson from Anime Awakening: working on textures and lighting simultaneously causes reflective whites problems. Lock lighting first, then surfaces, then details.
12.1 The 6-Phase Revamp Plan
Phase 1 — Lock Global Lighting
Set all Lighting properties, Atmosphere, and Sky. Do not touch again after this point.
Lighting: Ambient, Brightness, ColorShift_Top/Bottom, EnvironmentDiffuseScale, EnvironmentSpecularScale 0.08, Technology Future.
Atmosphere: Density 0.35, Color #2a1a4a, Offset 0.1.
Sky: dark void/abyss skybox — the floating fortress feeling lives or dies in the sky.
Nothing changes global lighting after Phase 1. If something looks slightly off, fix the Part — not the global light.
Phase 2 — Place All Permanent Light Sources
Every PointLight and SpotLight from Section 7 goes in now, at its final position. Textures are designed against this lighting.
Crown beam PointLight #8B2EFF Range 35.
Throne SpotLight gold Range 20.
All 4 wing entrance SpotLights.
Portal PointLight #8B2EFF Range 25 Brightness 3.
Training gate crimson SpotLights.
Phase 3 — Apply Floor SurfaceAppearance
The 96×96 floor with cracked dark marble is the test surface for the entire lighting setup. If it looks correct — dark, textured, picks up violet neon glow, no blown-out patches — everything else will work. If the floor looks wrong, fix lighting before touching anything else.
Phase 4 — Apply SurfaceAppearance to Hub, Throne, Walls
In order of player visibility: central hub corruption-veined stone, throne, corner buttresses, outer walls. Use Add Easy Textures plugin. Use texture move tool to fix seams on walls after applying.
Phase 5 — Gold Trim and Steel — Anti-Reflective Check
Apply aged brass SurfaceAppearance to all arch frames and trim strips. Then run the reflective whites test (temp PointLight Brightness 4 near the crown beam). Lower Reflectance on any piece that blows out. Remove temp light. This phase cannot be rushed.
Phase 6 — Final Detail Pass
Upload and place all decals (spawn sigil, crown banner, portal glyph). Add Fabric banners. Scatter debris with Brush Tool. Add corruption waterfall cascade at floor edges with Generate Waterfall plugin. Add all neon strips last — exactly as defined in Section 8.

16. Sightline & Navigation Rules
Lesson from Anime Awakening and Anime Adventures: every key area must be visible from the spawn point. Players should never feel lost.
16.1 Spawn Sightline Audit
Stand at the spawn dais. Look north toward the throne and crown beam — that view must be completely unobstructed.
From spawn you must be able to see: the crown beam top, the throne silhouette, at least 2 wing entrance arches, and the leaderboard wall.
Secondary towers on the N–S axis must be moved to the diagonal (NE, NW, SE, SW) — never directly in the spawn forward view.
Keep all paths visually open — no parts overhanging lower than 10 studs on path corridors.
Guild Hall and Ranked Portal entrances must be readable from spawn — use SpotLights above each entrance to make them pop.
16.2 Placement Rules
Put the crown centerpiece dead center.
Put the throne slightly behind center so it frames the hub, not blocks it.
Place 4 wings at equal 90-degree spacing around the center.
Keep all portals at least 8 studs away from the main ring edge.
Never put two tall towers directly in front of each other from the spawn view.
Limit the scene to 6–8 major towers, not dozens of random vertical pieces.
Use neon strips only to guide movement or highlight important interaction points.
Leave 6–8 studs of open walking space between all major objects.
